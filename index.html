01 / The big priority

Get a home, with a plan.

Rentals can move quickly. Prepare one strong application and search across several channels before you arrive.

01

Define your search

Set a monthly budget including charges, your move-in date and non-negotiables. Compare commute times on the CTS network, including nearby Eurométropole towns.

02

Prepare your dossier

Collect the documents a landlord can legally request. DossierFacile helps create a shareable rental file. Check whether Visale can act as guarantor.

03

Search, visit, verify

Try agencies, direct listings, flatshares and Maison de l'habitat. Visit before committing when possible; check the lease, energy rating, charges and inventory.

02 / Arrival essentials

Your first week, made simpler.

Some steps depend on your nationality, employment and household. Use the official pages to check your situation.

01 / ADMIN

Register your essentials

Use the city's newcomer guide for local services. For visas, residence, health cover, taxes and other national procedures, start at Service-Public.fr.

02 / HOME

Set up the address

Arrange home insurance before moving in, then utilities and internet. Keep copies of the signed lease, entry inventory and payment receipts.

03 / MOVEMENT

Learn the routes

Use CTS for tram, bus, tickets and live changes. Explore cycle options through Vélhop.

04 / FAMILY

School and care

If arriving with children, check school and after-school registration. Find local healthcare information through the city and national services.

ON TWO WHEELS

See the city by bike.

For a first ride, choose a marked route and allow time at tram crossings and busy pedestrian streets.

RENT

Try Vélhop first

Strasbourg's public bike service offers short and longer rentals. Check bike availability, deposits and current prices before booking.

Bike rental and stations ↗
ROUTES

Pick an easy ride

Use the official cycle map for daily trips. For a longer outing, explore the canal paths or the cross-border Piste des Forts.

Cycle routes and map ↗
EVERYDAY

Ride thoughtfully

Lock your bike securely, watch for tram rails, and slow down around the city centre's shared spaces. Check the route before a first commute.

City cycling guidance ↗
FOOD & ERRANDS

Find your everyday places.

Start with a market, a nearby supermarket and one café or bakery you like. Simple routines help a new neighborhood feel familiar.

EAT

Try Alsatian food

Share a tarte flambée, try a winstub for choucroute or baeckeoffe, and look for a bakery's lunch special. Check menus and prices before sitting down near the busiest sights.

Tourism office food guide ↗
GROCERIES

Shop by your routine

Use a neighborhood supermarket for essentials and outdoor markets for seasonal produce. Boulevard de la Marne and Neudorf are useful places to start; verify opening days before going.

Find a local market ↗
SHOPPING

Know where to go

The centre around Grande Île and Les Halles is handy for general shopping. Look to neighborhood shops for daily errands, and check Sunday opening hours in advance.
